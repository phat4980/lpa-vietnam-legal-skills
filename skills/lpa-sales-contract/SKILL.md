# LPA B2B Sales Contract

## Business model

Core transaction:

```text
Customer order
    ↓
Order confirmation
    ↓
Factory production/packing
    ↓
Delivery
    ↓
Customer receives/signs
    ↓
Invoice / reconciliation
    ↓
Payment
```

Possible channels:

- Zalo
- phone
- email
- customer PO
- customer portal
- framework agreement + recurring orders

## Contract should define

### Product

- purified bagged ice
- packaging/unit
- weight/quantity
- quality specification
- permitted product variants

Do not hard-code a product specification that the business has not confirmed.

### Ordering

For recurring B2B customers define:

- accepted channels
- who may place orders
- order information
- cut-off time
- confirmation method
- changes/cancellation
- evidence retained

### Delivery

Define:

- delivery address
- schedule
- delivery window
- driver/delivery evidence
- receiver
- shortage/damage procedure
- acceptance
- risk transfer

### Quality and complaints

Define:

- objective quality requirements
- receiving inspection
- complaint period
- evidence
- replacement/refund mechanism
- escalation

### Payment

Define:

- invoice basis
- reconciliation
- due date
- bank transfer/cash if applicable
- credit period
- late-payment treatment

Customer-specific credit terms must not be copied between customers without approval.

### Penalty/damages

Do not insert generic penalty language without checking the applicable legal regime and commercial structure.

## LPA operating policy

Company facts, pricing, credit limits, delivery windows, customer-specific terms, and approval authority belong in company policy files—not in the legal knowledge layer.

## Customer-specific appendix

For chains or large accounts, prefer an appendix/order schedule for:

- store/site list
- delivery calendar
- SKU
- price
- quantity
- contact
- receiving procedure
- payment terms

This keeps the master contract stable while commercial details can change.

## Default risk questions

Before signature, confirm:

1. Who can place an order?
2. What evidence proves an order?
3. When is an order binding?
4. When does risk transfer?
5. How is shortage recorded?
6. How long can the customer complain?
7. What happens to rejected product?
8. When is invoice issued?
9. When does payment become due?
10. What happens if payment is late?
11. Can either side suspend deliveries?
12. What happens when the contract ends?
